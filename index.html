The physics behind it

The key results of general relativity that the simulator reproduces.

Einstein field equations

Gμν = (8πG/c⁴) Tμν

Matter and energy tell spacetime how to curve; curved spacetime tells matter how to move.

Schwarzschild radius

rs = 2GM / c²

The horizon radius. Squeeze any mass inside it and you get a black hole. The Sun’s is 2.95 km.

Orbital precession

Δφ = 6πGM / (c² a (1−e²))

Elliptical orbits rotate each lap. This explains Mercury’s extra 43″ per century.

Light bending

δ = 4GM / (c² b)

Twice the Newtonian value. Gravitational lensing confirmed it during the 1919 eclipse.

Time dilation

dτ/dt = √(1 − rs/r)

Clocks run slower deeper in gravity, and stop at the horizon as seen from far away.

Orbit equation used

a = −(GM/r³)(1 + 3L²/(c²r²)) r

The extra 3L² term is the general-relativistic correction. Switch it off in the simulator to get Newton’s closed ellipses.

How to use it

  1. Pick a model from the menu in the simulator.
  2. Click the fabric to summon a planet. Adjust its launch speed first, or use Speed up / Slow down latest afterwards.
  3. Switch to Light ray mode or press Light beam to see gravitational lensing.
  4. Toggle the GR correction to compare Einstein with Newton.
  5. Slow a planet down until it crosses the red event horizon and disappears.

About this project

A college project exploring general relativity through an interactive 3D visualization. The spacetime fabric is the Flamm paraboloid, the true spatial geometry of a Schwarzschild black hole. Particles follow Schwarzschild geodesics integrated in real time in the browser.

Simplifications

Single non-rotating body, orbits confined to one plane, geometric units (G = c = 1), compressed distances and exaggerated well depth in the star models. Precession and light bending are correct to first order.

Built with

HTML, CSS, JavaScript and Three.js (WebGL). No build step and no server needed.
